# Welcome Area — Progression Arc

Status: design baseline for FORGE I content buildout

## Purpose

Welcome is the player's first lived-in multiplayer settlement and first compact progression chapter. It should teach the player how the world works through ordinary village problems rather than explicit tutorial prompts.

Target feel:

- the settlement initially feels small, functional, safe enough, and temporary only in the background;
- the player learns movement, combat, pickup/collection, equipment awareness, investigation, NPC relationships, and basic quest flow through useful work;
- ordinary logistical problems slowly accumulate into evidence that something outside Welcome is wrong;
- the player leaves on a reasonable, limited assignment rather than because an NPC announces a world-ending crisis;
- later, the player can return to the same geographical area in a ruined/changed world state for narrative closure.

This document supersedes the earlier idea that Welcome is permanently unreachable after departure. The revised direction is: the original living Welcome chapter ends when the player leaves, but the location itself may be revisited later in a materially changed or ruined state.

## Pacing target

The current design target remains approximately 1–2 hours for a player who engages with the main Welcome content.

Do not manufacture playtime through repeated `go out -> do one tiny objective -> return` loops. Prefer 3–4 meaningful excursions, each containing several compatible objectives.

Exact travel time, enemy density, XP values, rewards, and combat difficulty remain unfrozen until the relevant gameplay/content systems are ready for tuning.

## Existing village anchors

### Traveler / inn host

A recurring social and logistics anchor. Holds the workshop package, offers local context, and helps make caravan traffic feel ordinary before the important caravan fails to arrive.

### Workshop Craftsman

Practical repair/equipment anchor. Receives the workshop package and establishes that local work depends on small supply chains.

### Shopkeeper

General-goods and scarcity perspective. The shop is intentionally limited; the important caravan is expected to replenish practical supplies. No full commerce runtime is implied by the authored dialogue yet.

### Gate Watchman

Road-observer and early fieldwork anchor. He teaches through useful jobs rather than tutorial language and becomes the main bridge between village life and the increasingly suspicious state of the road.

## Gate Watchman fieldwork arc

The Watchman's progression should feel like the player gradually earns trust and range.

### Excursion 1 — Clear and collect near the road

One trip should contain several objectives rather than three separate returns.

Candidate shape:

- kill roughly 5 of a small road pest near the first marker;
- kill roughly 8–10 of a second, slightly tougher enemy farther along the same route;
- collect roughly 3 useful drops from the first enemy for local practical use;
- normal combat kills and quest completion both contribute to the player's early XP progression.

The exact enemy definitions, counts, drops, XP, and spawn locations are not frozen. The important design rule is that this is the player's first real combat-work session, not a one-kill tutorial.

### Excursion 2 — Follow and inspect the road markers

The next trip combines collection and investigation:

- gather scattered marker cloth or equivalent road-maintenance material;
- continue to the outer marker;
- inspect why it no longer sits correctly;
- discover that the marker was turned rather than simply broken.

This is the first small piece of information that does not fit an entirely routine explanation.

The Watchman's reaction should stay grounded: wind breaks things; it does not normally turn a marker around.

## Outer Guard / field patrol NPC

Introduce a second guard outside the village, positioned in a relatively safe field location on the route toward the Old Watch Post.

This NPC is not a replacement quest hub and does not require the player to return to Welcome between tasks. Their purpose is to create one concentrated field-combat session and make the route outward feel occupied by real village work.

### Suggested local training/work set

Approximately five objectives can be completed around the same field area, with short returns to the Outer Guard rather than full trips back to the village:

1. clear a small group around the guard's immediate operating area;
2. thin a larger group farther out;
3. collect several useful drops from local enemies;
4. recover a dropped pack/tool/supply from an enemy-held patch of terrain;
5. clear the approach toward the Old Watch Post, ending with either a tougher enemy or a denser encounter.

The intent is primarily combat repetition with enough variation that the player learns spacing, approach, health pressure, loot, and basic route awareness naturally.

After this session, the Outer Guard explicitly judges the player ready to continue toward the Old Watch Post.

### Defensive reward

A small defensive reward is appropriate here because the player is about to enter the most dangerous part of the Welcome field arc.

Do not freeze a shield reward yet: the current equipment model has no dedicated shield/off-hand slot. A defensive bodywear item, reinforced clothing, or another reward compatible with the existing equipment slots is safer until equipment design changes.

## Old Watch Post

The Old Watch Post should be a payoff location, not a container holding a quest item.

The player goes there to recover the Watchman's old signal lantern, but discovers that something has occupied or nested inside the structure.

Desired experience:

- approach is earned through the Outer Guard field session;
- enemies or environmental evidence make the post feel abandoned for a reason;
- inside is a stronger encounter, unusual creature, or light mini-boss appropriate for early-game difficulty;
- the player recovers the signal lantern after resolving the encounter;
- the post contains evidence that the occupant has been there for some time.

Exact creature identity, encounter layout, map topology, loot, and boss mechanics remain unfrozen.

## Reporting the Watch Post discovery

The player must be allowed to tell the Gate Watchman what happened, rather than the NPC automatically knowing it.

Core conversation shape:

- Player: found the lantern;
- Watchman asks what else happened;
- Player reports that something had made a nest or lair inside the old post and appeared to have been there for a while;
- Watchman reacts that this is not a good sign;
- he reveals that the village had considered putting people back at the post or expanding its use;
- that plan is now abandoned or reconsidered.

The Watchman should still speak cautiously. One occupied building is a problem, not proof of catastrophe.

## Accumulating evidence

By this point the player may have independently encountered several warning signs:

- unusual or increasing hostile presence near the road;
- an outer marker turned rather than merely damaged;
- the Old Watch Post occupied for some time;
- the important caravan late or missing;
- no expected dust, bells, advance runner, or other normal signs of arrival.

No single item should trigger a melodramatic conclusion. The significance comes from accumulation.

A suitable Watchman framing is essentially: any one of these things is ordinary enough; he is beginning to dislike how many ordinary explanations are required at once.

## Village Head

A Village Head / settlement leader should become relevant only after the player has gathered enough practical information to justify escalation.

Character direction:

- responsible and rational;
- wants ordinary explanations to remain sufficient;
- does not dismiss the player, but resists declaring a crisis prematurely;
- treats a late caravan, animal movement, damaged markers, and an abandoned post as individually explainable;
- changes position when the evidence begins to form a pattern.

The player should actively report the accumulated information. This is another opportunity to make dialogue reflect what the player actually discovered rather than giving NPCs omniscient knowledge.

## Final Welcome assignment / transition to the main world

The Village Head's response should remain practical and limited.

Preferred direction:

- prepare a sealed report, warning, or request for information;
- ask the player to take it to the nearest city, road office, outpost, or other established authority;
- ask whether the missing caravan passed their side or whether they know of road trouble;
- the Village Head expects the player to return with an answer.

This becomes the player's believable reason to leave Welcome and cross into the main world.

The character does not leave because they have been declared a chosen hero. They leave because, after a sequence of small useful jobs, they are now one of the people trusted to carry information beyond the settlement.

The older workshop-package concept should remain a local village quest and relationship-building thread, not the primary reason the player exits Welcome.

## Departure state

The living Welcome chapter ends when the player crosses into the main world on the Village Head assignment.

The departure should preserve unresolved expectations:

- the player expects to be able to report back;
- NPCs may speak as though they expect to see the player again;
- the missing caravan remains a practical concern rather than fully explained catastrophe;
- the player has enough competence and gear to survive the next chapter, but is still clearly early in progression.

## Later return — Ruined Welcome

Revised design decision: Welcome is not permanently inaccessible.

At a later point in the main game, the player may return to the same geographical area, but not to the original safe Welcome state.

The returned area should provide closure through contrast:

- recognizable geography, buildings, road markers, workshop/inn/shop locations, and other visual anchors remain identifiable;
- damage, collapse, occupation, environmental changes, or other consequences show that the place has materially changed;
- NPC presence and state may differ substantially;
- old quest spaces can acquire different traversal, enemies, interactions, or meaning;
- objects and dialogue from the first chapter can be recontextualized rather than simply repeated;
- the player should recognize places where they previously performed mundane work and understand what was lost or changed.

This return is not intended to restore Welcome as the original beginner hub. It is a later world-state version of the location and should support narrative closure, discovery, and potentially new content.

## Closure opportunities

Potential later callbacks, not yet frozen:

- revisit the Gate Watchman's post or road and discover what became of the watch;
- revisit the Old Watch Post after understanding more about what occupied it;
- find the workshop and connect its earlier practical repair work to the aftermath;
- discover what happened to the inn/Traveler, Shopkeeper, Craftsman, Outer Guard, and Village Head individually rather than resolving everyone through one exposition dump;
- recover, recognize, or reuse an object from the original Welcome chapter;
- allow the player to understand that the first chapter's ordinary errands were early symptoms of a wider event.

The emotional goal is recognition and consequence, not simply nostalgia.

## Current content sequence — working baseline

A likely high-level order is:

1. arrive / orient within Welcome;
2. meet core village NPCs and complete local package/social content;
3. Gate Watchman first field excursion: combat + drops;
4. second excursion: marker collection + investigation;
5. meet Outer Guard;
6. complete concentrated Outer Guard combat/work set;
7. earn a small defensive preparation reward;
8. enter Old Watch Post and recover the lantern after a stronger encounter;
9. report the occupied post and other observations to Gate Watchman;
10. escalate accumulated intelligence to Village Head;
11. receive practical message/investigation assignment to the next settlement;
12. leave Welcome and enter the main world;
13. much later, return to the ruined/changed Welcome area for closure.

This is a progression baseline, not a requirement that every player interaction occur in exactly this linear order. Existing flexible dialogue principles should still allow sensible variation where state permits it.

## Runtime / tooling boundary

The authored NPC files must not become a hidden generic quest manager.

Dialogue may request work and react to facts/items, but authoritative game systems should own actual gameplay truth such as:

- kill counts and enemy identity;
- XP awards;
- item drops and collection counts;
- world-location discovery;
- encounter completion;
- area/world-state transitions;
- rewards and equipment ownership;
- persistence.

NPC dialogue consumes those results through durable state/facts and presents the appropriate conversation.

## Deliberately unfrozen

Do not lock these until their owning systems/content are ready:

- final names/species of early enemies;
- exact kill and collection counts;
- exact XP curve and quest XP;
- exact defensive reward;
- shield/off-hand support;
- Outer Guard name/background/final dialogue;
- Village Head name/background/final dialogue;
- nearest city/outpost identity and geography;
- Old Watch Post map/layout and mini-boss identity;
- exact timing/cause/state of Welcome's destruction;
- which NPCs survive, leave, disappear, or die;
- exact trigger and timing for the player's later return.

The stable part is the progression shape and narrative function: ordinary useful work -> growing field competence -> accumulating evidence -> practical escalation -> departure -> later transformed return and closure.
